# Spectral theory and many-body catalogue audit

Review date: 4 September 2026. Baseline: commit `e32e461`. Scope: all 23 entries M1–M11 and B1–B12. This is a review artifact, not a change to the catalogue. Recommendations are editorial judgments unless explicitly identified as mathematical corrections.

## Findings requiring attention

1. **M2 incorrectly presents general two-dimensional Anderson localization as proved.** The full-spectrum, arbitrarily weak-disorder problem for the standard scalar i.i.d. model on the square lattice is itself a major open question. One-dimensional localization and two-dimensional band-edge/strong-disorder results must be distinguished. The introduction of [Bucaj et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06135) explicitly identifies the 2D problem; [Hurtado's 2026 paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-026-05559-8) extends bottom-of-spectrum localization to nonstationary potentials, not all energies. This error has also hidden a worthwhile missing problem.

2. **B12 gives the wrong linear-light-cone threshold.** In the usual worst-case operator-norm setting, strict linear cones are established for decay exponent alpha > 2d+1, not simply alpha > 2d. For 2d < alpha < 2d+1 the optimal propagation exponent is different. [Tran et al., PRL 127, 160401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15828) already settles the principal exponents for alpha > 2d. Rewrite around the remaining regimes and explicitly specify interaction normalization, norm, error tolerance, and whether arbitrary time-dependent protocols are allowed. Do not count the already settled regime as a new open target.

3. **B1 needs explicit ground-space assumptions.** A gap alone cannot imply an area law for every vector in an unrestricted degenerate ground space. For example, put on-site projectors on one sublattice and leave the other spins free: the excitation gap is one, but the free spins can form a volume-entangled ground state. A clean conjecture uses uniformly bounded finite-range terms, fixed on-site dimension, a uniform gap and a unique ground state, with a precise choice of regions and uniform constant. Also, the known frustration-free theorem assumes a *local* gap and a specified cut geometry; it is not a theorem for every frustration-free 2D Hamiltonian. [Anshu–Arad–Gosset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02492).

4. **B2/B3 conflate three different goals: representation, contraction, and finding the representation.** B2 does not say in which variables the bond dimension is polynomial, or whether error is global or only local. B3 claims efficiency from a boundary gap/finite correlation length too casually, and a hardness theorem does not unconditionally prove that no polynomial algorithm exists. The useful question is a stated promise class and a proven runtime/error bound. [Schwarz–Buerschaper–Eisert](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06301) gives a quasi-polynomial local-observable result under particular assumptions; [Molnár et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2973) supplies distinct Gibbs-state and ground-state approximation guarantees.

5. **M7 conflates injectivity with G-injectivity.** Ordinary injectivity and the symmetry-enriched generalization do not both imply a unique ground state on a torus. G-injective constructions explicitly accommodate topological degeneracy. Also, undecidability for unrestricted Hamiltonians should not be transferred to an arbitrary promised PEPS family without identifying the relevant reduction. [Schuch–Cirac–Pérez-García, “PEPS as ground states: Degeneracy and topology”](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003491610000990).

6. **M4 overstates the avalanche argument.** The cited [De Roeck–Huveneers paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.01815) develops an ETH-based theory and predictions. It is not an unconditional proof that MBL is impossible in all d >= 2 systems. [Imbrie's result](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.7837) has its explicit level-statistics assumption. The remaining question needs a Hamiltonian ensemble, energy window, definition of localization, probability statement and perturbation class.

7. **M8/M10 mix a false unrestricted conjecture with a valid restricted frontier.** Ergodicity alone does not imply QUE: [Hassell](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.0666) gives ergodic billiard counterexamples. Under the hypotheses of the quantum ergodicity theorem, a positive-density family with a fixed non-Liouville limiting measure is already excluded. Exceptional zero-density sequences, quantitative scarring, quasimodes and nonergodic classical systems are different questions. M8 should use a concrete negatively curved/Anosov geometric class; M10 should state which of these remaining notions of scarring it concerns. See also [Dyatlov's mathematical account](https://www.ams.org/journals/notices/202310/noti2801/noti2801.html).

8. **B8's 1D classification summary suppresses important assumptions.** Specify bosonic versus fermionic systems, symmetry breaking and unique versus degenerate ground spaces. A gapped path induces quasi-local spectral flow; exact finite-depth-circuit equality is not an unrestricted equivalence without approximation and stabilization conventions. [Schuch–Pérez-García–Cirac](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3732) carefully separates these cases. B9 is worthwhile if narrowed to a concrete completeness/realizability conjecture instead of repeating B8's entire programme.

9. **B11 includes an unrelated/misattributed reference.** “Simulating quantum many-body dynamics on a current digital quantum computer” is not the primary source for the topological intrinsic-sign-problem claim. Relevant original works are [Smith–Golan–Ringel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05343) and [Golan–Smith–Ringel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05566). Their assumptions and criteria do not justify an unqualified assertion about every phase with nonzero chiral central charge.

10. **M1 misses relevant 2025 progress.** [Hundertmark–Pattakos–Schulz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18487) reports the explicit bound N_c(Z) < 1.1185 Z + O(Z^(1/3)). This improves a particular explicit all-Z bound, not the asymptotic coefficient-one result already cited and not the ionization conjecture. The question itself remains an excellent catalogue entry. [Solovej's 2025 lectures](https://bruneau.perso.math.cnrs.fr/quantissima2025/slidesweek2/Solovej_Quantissima2025_Lectures.pdf) formulate the related uniform excess-charge, radius and ionization-energy questions.

## Important omissions and replacement candidates

### Priority 1: concrete questions fitting the existing scope

**Two-dimensional Anderson localization at arbitrary disorder.** For a fixed nontrivial bounded i.i.d. scalar potential law on Z^2, prove almost-sure localization throughout the spectrum for every nonzero disorder strength; distinguish spectral localization from the desired quantitative dynamical bounds. This is complementary to M2, not a mobility-edge duplicate. Primary starting points: [Bucaj et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06135), introduction, and the scope of [Hurtado 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-026-05559-8).

**Bose–Einstein condensation in the homogeneous interacting gas at fixed density.** For a specified repulsive finite-range 3D Bose gas at zero temperature and sufficiently small fixed gas parameter, prove a macroscopic eigenvalue of the one-body density matrix as N,L grow with N/L^3 fixed. This differs from the Gross–Pitaevskii/mean-field scaling limits and from proving the energy expansion. [Solovej's 2025 original open-problem account](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/physique/item/10.5802/crphys.247.pdf), sections 5 and 8, explicitly identifies the distinction. Positive-temperature condensation can be a separate, harder subproblem.

**Laughlin bulk spectral gap for the untruncated parent Hamiltonian.** Prove a system-size-independent gap above the ground space of the fixed-filling fermionic nu=1/3 Haldane pseudopotential on tori of bounded aspect ratio, with energy normalization fixed. Avoid conflating a bulk energy gap, an entanglement-spectrum gap, and a thin-cylinder truncation. The [Warzel–Young thin-torus theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13764) is a restricted Hamiltonian-gap advance. [Schraven–Warzel 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12165) concerns thin-cylinder correlation decay and an entanglement-spectrum gap, not a Hamiltonian spectral-gap theorem. Neither settles the full question. [Young's bulk-gap account](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01405) supplies a useful route.

**Sharp Lieb–Thirring constants.** A focused entry can ask whether the optimal first-moment constant L_(1,3) equals the semiclassical constant for scalar Schrödinger operators on R^3; an incremental companion can track certified bounds. The old all-dimension/all-exponent two-candidate conjecture must not be copied indiscriminately: [Frank–Gontier–Lewin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04964) disproved part of it. [Frank–Hundertmark–Jex–Nam](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09017) gives improved bounds, with [Schimmer's review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06051) mapping the surviving conjectures. This is more directly spectral than M11.

**Ground-state phase of the spin-1/2 nearest-neighbor kagome Heisenberg antiferromagnet.** Determine the thermodynamic phase and excitation gap for the precisely specified uniform model, with controlled finite-size/numerical error or a proof. Distinguish it from material-specific additional couplings and from square-kagome or breathing-kagome variants. The 2026 primary study [Jiang et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-026-01959-5) explicitly describes the gapped-Z2 versus gapless-Dirac dispute. This supplies a concrete quantum-spin-liquid question currently absent from the broad B8/B9 coverage.

**Robust, initializable three-dimensional passive quantum memories.** Add only with the current frontier attached. [Balasubramanian–Davydova–Lin, May 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10943) claims a geometrically local bounded-density 3D CSS construction with stretched-exponential lifetime under its thermal dynamics. Suitable residual targets are robustness of the memory lifetime under a specified perturbation class and efficient passive initialization. In v1, the TQO-2 argument needed for the proposed perturbative gap-stability result and the full lifetime proof for the deterministic embedding are explicitly deferred: these belong on a proof-status watchlist, not automatically on a list of established open problems. The generic existence question cannot honestly be introduced as simply untouched. Hamiltonian-gap stability alone would not prove stability of memory lifetime. This is passive storage, distinct from A18's active fault tolerance.

### Priority 2: further breadth after the repairs

**Homogeneous electron gas / Wigner crystallization.** At a stated dimension, spin sector and low density, prove or refute crystalline order for quantum jellium and characterize its competing fluid phases. This is distinct from the lattice Hubbard model. [Lewin's 2025 open-problem contribution](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/physique/item/CRPHYS_2025__26_G1_369_0/) directly identifies the homogeneous electron gas as a frontier. It needs a further model-specific literature pass before publishing a sharp crystallization statement.

**Unitary Fermi gas.** The equal-mass two-component zero-range gas deserves at least one operational target, such as certified bounds on the Bertsch parameter or transport with specified errors. Use [Castin's 2025 open questions](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/physique/item/CRPHYS_2025__26_G1_393_0/) to select the formulation; do not turn the whole field into another catch-all card.

**Further candidates to scope before adding:** stability/classification of driven or monitored many-body phases, mixed-state topological order, rigorously controlled Fermi-liquid behavior, and quantum many-body state preparation. These are coverage gaps, but their exact questions need to be selected rather than treating every active field as automatically deserving a new entry. The [IHP/Comptes Rendus collection](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/physique/item/CRPHYS_2024__25_S6/) is a useful researcher-authored cross-check on breadth.

## Limits of this review

Every assigned catalogue statement and context was read. Mathematical scope and bibliography were cross-checked using the linked original papers and researcher-authored accounts; selected full-text statements were inspected for disputed boundaries. This is not an independent proof verification of the cited research or an exhaustive systematic review of every paper in these fields. Absence of a discovered resolution is not proof that none exists. In particular, new 2025–2026 preprints remain attributed claims unless publication or independent validation was established. Final wording for B2/B3, M6/M7 and the broad classification programmes merits specialist review before publication.
