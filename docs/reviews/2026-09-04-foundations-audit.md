# Catalogue audit: QFT, open systems, and foundations

Audit date: 4 September 2026. Scope: all 30 entries in `cat-qft.js` (QF1–QF10), `cat-open-systems.js` (O1–O9), and `cat-foundations.js` (F1–F11). These files were read in full. This is a research/editorial review; no catalogue data were changed.

The main weakness is not that these research areas have become irrelevant. It is that several entries identify the open problem as the absence of a formalism that already exists, or the absence of universal agreement, while omitting the precise remaining mathematical or experimental question. Several substantive 2024–2026 results also predate the last update and materially change the starting point.

`Keep`, `reframe`, and `merge` below are editorial recommendations, not claims of community consensus. A theorem in an arXiv manuscript is explicitly identified as a preprint result. “Partly answered” does not mean the entire research area is solved.

## Highest-priority scientific corrections

### 1. O6 misses a direct answer to a large part of its stated question

Shiraishi and Takagi's **preprint** [Recovery of the second law in fully quantum thermodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05642), submitted 7 October 2025, addresses thermal operations themselves, not merely Gibbs-preserving operations. Its main theorem states that, provided the target's resonant coherent modes are contained in those of the input, conversion with an exactly restored correlated catalyst and arbitrarily accurate output is possible iff the free energy does not increase. The final system–catalyst correlation can be arbitrarily small. The theorem's assumptions were checked in the full-text main statement, not inferred solely from its broad abstract.

This does **not** settle catalyst-free conversion, exactly correct outputs, bounded catalyst size/energy, or removal of the mode condition. O6 should explicitly separate those frontiers from the characterized approximate correlated-catalytic regime. A related **published theorem** for covariant Gibbs-preserving operations appeared in [Shiraishi, PRL 134, 160402 (2025)](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.160402); that larger operation class must not be conflated with thermal operations.

### 2. F9 omits a much more direct physical-realizability result than its latest update

Salzger and Vilasini's **preprint** [Higher-order quantum processes respecting closed labs in a spacetime have quantum controlled causal order](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08351), submitted 8 May 2026, derives a characterization under explicit assumptions: classical acyclic background spacetime, the causal-box framework, and fine-grained closed-laboratory conditions called Acting Once and Local Order. Processes satisfying these conditions are behaviorally equivalent to QC-QCs. Thus a substantial version of the question in F9 has a proposed answer, not just a refined classification of correlations.

Retain an open boundary question about relaxing the laboratory assumptions, continuous/relativistic implementations, or moving beyond classical background spacetime. Do not state that **all** indefinite causal order is impossible in spacetime, and do not interpret this theorem as an unrestricted characterization of quantum gravity. Earlier **published** no-go results already distinguished localized laboratories from delocalized implementations: [Vilasini and Renner, Fundamental limits for realising quantum processes in spacetime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13387).

There is also a definite bibliographic error: the Mothe–Abbott–Branciard paper with DOI `10.1103/bt67-n8kx` is listed by APS as [PRX Quantum, accepted 20 August 2026](https://journals.aps.org/prxquantum/accepted/10.1103/bt67-n8kx), not *Physical Review Research*. The catalogue's “Published progress (August 2026)” should not silently turn an acceptance date into a publication date.

### 3. F3 overstates what experiments establish about real quantum theory

[Renou et al., Nature 600 (2021)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04160-4) prove a distinction for a specific real-Hilbert-space theory with the specified composition and independent-source assumptions. This is not an assumption-free proof that every real-number formulation is experimentally impossible. The cited article is a theoretical proposal for an experiment, not itself the experimental realization described in the catalogue.

A **published 2026 result**, [Barrios Hita et al., PRL 136, 240202](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/4k13-sdjh), constructs a real-number description reproducing multipartite predictions after changing the composite-system postulate. A separate **preprint**, [Hoffreumon and Woods, arXiv:2603.19208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19208), distinguishes operational independence from product-state independence and proves simulation results under the former. Neither refutes Renou's theorem with its original assumptions. They show why the catalogue must make those assumptions explicit.

### 4. QF8's operational theory is much further developed than the entry suggests

[van Luijk, Stottmeister, Werner and Wilming, Pure State Entanglement and von Neumann Algebras, CMP (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-025-05465-5) develops LOCC for commuting von Neumann algebras and extends Nielsen's pure-state conversion theorem to arbitrary factors. Under its Haag-duality assumptions, type III pure states admit arbitrarily accurate LOCC conversion into one another; for type III_1 even classical communication is unnecessary. This is a **published mathematical result**, not merely a proposal for a formalism.

The useful frontier is operational restrictions: finite energy, spacetime localization, preparation cost, mixed states, and gauge constraints. Universal embezzlement illustrates why these restrictions matter: [Embezzlement of entanglement, quantum fields, and the classification of von Neumann algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07299). Also correct the blanket claims that relative entropy is finite and mutual information is UV finite: these require hypotheses; algebraic relative entropy can take the value infinity, and touching regions need not have finite mutual information. [Longo and Xu, Relative Entropy in CFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.07283), introduction and section 2.1, explicitly distinguishes these issues; its free-fermion interval formula diverges in the touching-interval limit.

### 5. F4 and F11 incorrectly describe positive frameworks as missing

F4 says that only no-go constraints are firm and a consistent multi-agent framework is missing. [Vilasini and Woods, arXiv:2209.09281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09281) explicitly presents a general quantum-circuit framework for logically and causally consistent reasoning in extended Wigner's-friend scenarios, without absolute measurement events. This is a **preprint framework**, not a consensus solution of the measurement problem. It nevertheless invalidates the unqualified absence claim. A better question concerns which assumptions, records, and agent interventions make the frameworks operationally distinguishable.

F11 omits the **published** [Trinity of relational quantum dynamics, PRD 104, 066001 (2021)](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.104.066001). It unifies relational Dirac observables, Page–Wootters dynamics, and reduced Heisenberg descriptions, using covariant POVMs that include nonideal clocks. The need is to identify remaining interactions/backreaction/finite-resource restrictions, not to request this unification as though it had never been done. Its overlap with F8 and O7 is substantial.

### 6. O3–O5 need a correct distinction between states, maps, and intervention processes

Strong coupling does not itself destroy complete positivity: unitary evolution followed by a partial trace gives a CPTP map from arbitrary system inputs whenever the initial environment is fixed and uncorrelated. Correlated preparations require a preparation domain or an intervention-based description. Requesting a CPTP map on **all** system states for **arbitrary** correlations can be incompatible with the premises; an exact process-tensor formalism does not suffer that same problem.

O4 misstates Pechukas: the linear, consistent, positive full-domain assignment is a fixed-product assignment; the conclusion is not that the interaction “coupling” must be trivial. The 2009 Shabani–Lidar citation must be accompanied by its [2016 erratum](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.049901). [Buscemi's 2014 theorem](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.140502) gives an important information-theoretic characterization with its own assumptions. It should not be oversold as resolving every possible restricted-domain problem: [Dominy, Shabani and Lidar](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0908) distinguish three inequivalent notions of complete positivity in this setting and explain limitations of discord and data-processing criteria.

O5 already cites the process-tensor programme but treats adoption of a definition as the remaining scientific task. [Pollock et al., Operational Markov condition for quantum processes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.09811) provides a necessary-and-sufficient intervention-based Markov condition and operational memory measures. The relevant open tasks are restricted-access memory detection, minimal realization/compression, and certified simulation cost. The verified [Pauli-twirling result](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-026-01345-5) concerns a separation involving semigroup embeddability; non-embeddability of a single channel must not automatically be described as multi-time operational memory.

### 7. QF5, QF6 and QF10 contain important scope problems

[Lüscher's abelian chiral-gauge construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9811032) is **nonperturbative** and includes finite-volume topological sectors. QF5's “to all orders” understates that result and conflates it with the harder nonabelian construction. General chiral gauge theories remain a valid programme, but the baseline must recognize the existing abelian solution. Recent developments include [Chiral lattice gauge theories from symmetry disentanglers, JHEP (July 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP07%282026%29271); a generic continuum construction is not established merely by citing a proposed regulator.

QF6's assertion of full asymptotic completeness for generic constructive P(phi)_2/2D–3D theories is too broad. The introduction of [Duell and Dybalski, CMP (2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-023-04690-0) explicitly distinguishes the first complete results for factorizing 1+1D models from its own higher-dimensional **wedge-local** models. Partial particle sectors or space-cutoff models must not be represented as full asymptotic completeness for local relativistic theories.

QF10's request for intrinsically distinguished states needs to recognize an obstruction, not only an unfinished construction: [Fewster and Verch](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.4785) prove a no-go theorem for covariant preferred-state assignments under dynamical locality and other stated assumptions. Restricted spacetime classes or additional preparation data are legitimate targets; a universally natural vacuum cannot be presented as merely awaiting discovery.

No entire research discipline is recommended for deletion merely because it lacks one universally accepted formulation. The formulations to retire are “find the unique agreed formalism,” “obtain consensus,” and “derive a preferred object without additional data” when a no-go theorem or multiple legitimate operational models explain why the demand is inappropriate.

## Verification limits and publication guidance

- All 30 source entries were read, and all received an individualized decision. The full catalogue's titles were scanned for overlap; the other sections are audited separately.
- Primary paper pages, arXiv records, publisher pages, Clay, and an author's original problem list support the material findings. Secondary aggregators were used only to discover primary sources, not as decisive evidence.
- Targeted full-text inspection was performed for the O6 conversion theorem, the F9 closed-laboratory characterization and scope, the QF8 operational theory's principal statements, the QFT-measurement omission, and the roadmap's non-Abelian questions. This is not a line-by-line proof verification of those papers.
- Several remaining items were verified at abstract/metadata level, particularly the newest 2026 references. The Shabani–Lidar erratum's existence and bibliographic relation were verified; this audit did not transcribe and reprove its corrected theorem. Its parent paper should not be used as an unqualified necessary-and-sufficient discord criterion.
- “No solution found” is not a proof that an open question remains open. Keep judgments on large programmes are based on the surviving formulation and cited current baseline, not an exhaustive search of every claimed proof.
- When revising the catalogue, distinguish a settled restricted theorem, a claimed preprint theorem, a remaining quantitative question, and an interpretational programme. A new reference alone should not automatically increment an “improved problem” count.
