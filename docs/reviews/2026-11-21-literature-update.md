## Answer to the maintainer's question

None of the analyses read finds a formal error in the deduction from the premises as Frauchiger and Renner state them. They argue that the deduction uses an unstated or unjustified premise:

- collapse applied by a friend to her own observation while the superobservers treat her laboratory unitarily (Sudbery 2017; Baumann–Hansen–Wolf 2016; Baumann–Wolf 2018; Lazarovici–Hubert 2019; Healey 2018, appendix);
- explicit Bohmian counter-models satisfying single outcomes and quantum statistics; Sudbery and Lazarovici–Hubert conclude that the theorem as stated is false;
- an implicit unitary-modelling assumption U (Nurgalieva–del Rio 2019, colleagues of Renner, framed as an amendment);
- insensitivity of counterfactuals to an isolated intervening measurement (Healey 2018);
- an inconsistent four-time family of histories (Losada–Laura–Lombardi 2019);
- Born-rule constraints on correlations no observer can access, with timing details judged inessential (Schmid–Yīng–Leifer, preprint).

The Local Friendliness theorem (Bong et al. 2020) is widely presented as the version with the clearest assumptions, and has been tested experimentally with photon, qubit-register and small-circuit friends.

## Corrections to the previous entry text

The previous Local Friendliness sentence was accurate. It now states that the LF and Bell polytopes coincide in the two-friend scenario with two settings per superobserver, and that the 2020 experiment was a proof of principle with loopholes open. The previous claim that interpretations deny "absoluteness … or agent-independence" was replaced by sourced statements, and the classification of interpretations is now attributed to its source, because Frauchiger–Renner (2018) and Nurgalieva–Renner (2020) disagree.
