## External contribution: issue #22 (QF5)

**QF5.** Issue #22 (opened 26 November 2026 by `Multi-DAC`, written by Clawd and posted from Clayton Iggulden-Schnell's account) links two releases in [`Multi-DAC/releases`](https://github.com/Multi-DAC/releases). The catalogue links both at commit `095e7de`, the version that was checked. Neither is on arXiv or peer reviewed. Both state that Clawd and Wren are AIs, and this is recorded in `provenance`.

The relevance to QF5 goes through Lüscher's construction. When the charges come in opposite-sign pairs and the bases are related by charge conjugation, the chiral partition function in the vacuum sector equals the Dirac overlap determinant over the positive charges, "the expected result for a vector-like theory" (arXiv:hep-lat/9811032, Section 3.6, eq. (3.19), checked in the source). A failure or recovery of reflection positivity for the vector-like overlap determinant therefore bears on the overlap-based chiral constructions already listed in QF5, as a special case.

*The finite-spacing witness* (`hep-lat/overlap-link-rp-2x2-witness`, 8 pages, read in full). For two-dimensional $U(1)$ lattice gauge theory on an $8\times1$ lattice, with Wilson kernel ($r=1$, $m=0.7$) and the plain Wilson plaquette action, the measure $\det D_{\rm ov}\,e^{-\beta S_G}\,dU$ is not link-reflection positive at any $\beta\ge0$. Two smooth gauge fields that differ in one spatial link give a reflected $2\times2$ Gram matrix $K$ with $v^TKv\in[-8.2647,-8.2322]\times10^{-9}$ for $v=(1,-1)$, enclosed by two independent interval-arithmetic programs. A smearing argument turns it into a smooth gauge-invariant observable. The reduction to one holonomy, the shared-boundary condition and the smearing step were checked by hand. An independent floating-point recomputation, written for this review from the paper's definitions and sharing no code with the release, reproduces $v^TKv=-8.2485\times10^{-9}$, the two negative eigenvalues $-6.794\times10^{-6}$ and $-3.009\times10^{-6}$ of the $4\times4$ Gram matrix at $\beta=0$, and the positive semidefinite Gram matrix of the Wilson determinant $\det(D_W-0.7)$. The interval enclosure itself was not re-run. The lattice has one spatial site, where spatial links are gauge invariant, so this is a finite-spacing statement about a very small system. It says nothing about larger lattices, four dimensions, non-Abelian groups or the continuum.

*The second-order recovery* (`hep-lat/dwf-overlap-link-rp-second-order`, about 30 pages, read in full). The paper expands $\log\det D_{\rm DW}-\log\det D_{\rm PV}$ to second order about the free field, for bulk mass $-m$ with $0<m<1$, and studies the quadratic form across a plane of links for fields at least $t_0$ rows from the plane. Part (a), at finite $L_5$ and $0<a_5\le1$, uses link reflection positivity of each five-dimensional determinant separately together with a spectral form of the Pauli–Villars term, and bounds the negative part by $\rho_{\rm PV}^{2(t_0-1)}(1-\rho_{\rm PV}^2)^{-1}\,\mathrm{tr}\,\tilde N_{\rm PV}$. This argument is elementary and was checked. Its constant grows with $L_5a_5$, numerically linearly. Part (b), Lemma L4, gives an inner-moment bound $C(m)(t+2)^4\rho_*^t$ uniform in $L_5$, $a_5$, the spatial size and the momentum. Its proof goes through a residue sum rule, interlacing of domain-wall and Pauli–Villars poles and a rank-two Woodbury bound. The structure was followed, but several constants, such as $C_1(m)$ in Lemma 4A, are asserted rather than computed. Part (c) passes to the overlap limit $a_5\to0$, $L_5a_5\to\infty$, with rates, by a fixed Dunford–Taylor contour, and gives $Q^{\rm ov}(\alpha)\ge-C\,t_0^4\rho_*^{2(t_0-1)}\|\alpha\|^2$. The paper's Section 6 lists the statements that are numerical only. The witness and the recovery are consistent: the second-order bound is $O(1)$ next to the plane, and the witness is non-perturbative.

Neither release changes the status or the residual of QF5. The chiral Weyl overlap, four dimensions, the non-perturbative kernel and the interacting continuum limit remain open. Secondary sources cited in the releases (Kikukawa and Usui, Kikukawa's YITP talk, Mandula, Creutz) were not read for this update and are not cited in the catalogue.

No antecedent search beyond the releases' own was needed, since the catalogue records the work as the contributors' progress and makes no priority claim.

## arXiv sweep of 24–26 November 2026

**E1 · NPT bound entanglement — Improved → Claimed solved (E2 progress).** Islam Faisal, *Bound Entanglement with Negative Partial Transpose Exists*, [arXiv:2611.34921v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34921v1) (submitted 25 November 2026; only v1 exists, comment "Initial version", not withdrawn), was read in full from its LaTeX source, appendices and bibliography included. It claims that the Werner state $(23I-3F)/1448$ on $\mathbb C^8\otimes\mathbb C^8$ ($d=8$, $\alpha=-3/23$ in the E1/E2 convention, inside the NPT one-copy-undistillable window) is NPT and that, for every number of copies, every two-qubit output of a separable operation has Bell fidelity at most $(1+\sqrt2)/4$; hence $E_D=0$, which is exactly the construction E1 asks for. Every step of Lemmas 1–6 and Appendix B was rechecked by hand: the tensorization of the four one-copy conditions (Lemma 4), the splitting $|u_n\rangle=|v\rangle+|w\rangle$, the coherence bound and its rescaling and tensor-power bootstrap (Lemma 5), and the passage from the fidelity bound to $E_D=0$ and to PPT rank-two projections. No gap was found. The only computational input, the certificate $Q$ of Lemma 3, was rebuilt from Appendix A with new code, and all four conditions ($J\pm Q\succeq0$, $Q^{\Gamma_{\rm in}}\succeq0$, $(J-Q)(|u\rangle\otimes I)=0$) were verified in exact integer arithmetic: by rational Gaussian elimination on every one of the 708 blocks of the sparsity pattern (block sizes 1, 3, 6, 22 and 42, as in the paper's Table I). A redundant second exact test (characteristic-polynomial signs on every distinct block) was started but stopped unfinished after 30 minutes, so it gives no result. $J-Q$ has a kernel of dimension exactly 64, as condition (d) forces. Lemma 1 and the factorization of Lemma 2 were also checked exactly. Negative controls confirm that the checker rejects non-positive matrices and that changing one coefficient by 1, or the partial transpose to another system, breaks the certificate. A floating-point check of Lemma 4 for one copy agrees: under local minimization over $|v\rangle$ (40 random starts) the block matrix $\Omega_{v,w}$ stays positive semidefinite, while it is indefinite for a generic pair with $|v\rangle+|w\rangle\ne|u\rangle$, so the restriction in Lemma 4 is genuinely used. The rest of that script (Lemma 4 for two copies, random Kraus tests of Lemmas 5 and 6, and a two-copy see-saw search for Schmidt-rank-two vectors) was stopped unfinished and gives no result. Not checked: the Corollary (non-additivity and non-convexity of $E_D$), which rests on published results of Eggeling–Vollbrecht–Werner–Wolf, Vollbrecht–Wolf and Shor–Smolin–Terhal that were not reread, and Lemma 3.2 of the OpenAI E16 manuscript, which the proof reproduces in full rather than citing. The paper also cites the withdrawn arXiv:0802.0329 (Hiroshima), unrefereed GitHub and Hexagon preprints (MacCormack–Bao; Tomar, 2611.00464v1 and 2611.00466v1) and two old claims (quant-ph/0608250, quant-ph/0609050), but only as history; the proof does not depend on any of them, and the catalogue cites none of them on this basis. The comparison with DiVincenzo, Shor, Smolin, Terhal and Thapliyal (PRA 61, 062312 (2000), read in full) confirms the setting: their Schmidt-rank-two criterion and canonical reduction, their result that the undistillable interval near the PPT boundary may shrink with $n$, and their conjecture, which this claim answers at one point. Two elementary consequences not stated in the paper were derived during this review (mixing with the separable boundary state would extend the claim to $-3/23\le\alpha<-1/8$ in $d=8$, and embedding followed by $U\otimes U$ twirling would give an undistillable NPT Werner state in every $d>8$); they are not recorded in the catalogue. Nothing follows for $3\le d\le7$. E2, the archived Werner benchmark, receives an undated progress note and a reworded residual. Priority search: issues and pull requests of `the-omega-institute/trureturing` were searched for 2611.34921, "NPT bound entanglement", "Werner", "undistillable", "distillable entanglement" and related terms. The only related item, open issue #13057, preregisters the three-copy Werner endpoint and contains only numerics, so it is not an antecedent. Web searches found no other all-copy claim. The paper's AI disclosure (generative AI from OpenAI and Anthropic and the agent memory ibsema.ai assisted with finding the state and writing the paper) is recorded in `provenance`. The status is "Claimed solved" because the preprint is unrefereed and no independent expert assessment exists; the author announces a revised version with verification code, which should be checked at the next update. No Lean formalization exists, so the entry has no Lean badge.

**N19 (single-copy saturability of the multiparameter quantum Cramér–Rao bound).** Conlon, Suzuki, Lam and Assad replaced arXiv:2208.07386 on 24 November 2026 with v4, posted with the comment “Significant updates and corrections”. **R3 (are RG flows gradient flows?).** Three same-week preprints were read from their LaTeX sources: Benfatto, Pannell and Stergiou, arXiv:2611.28205v1 (20 November, read in full including both appendices), and Reehorst, arXiv:2611.28206v1 (20 November, read in full including appendices A–E) and arXiv:2611.31050v1 (23 November, 245 pages; read in full, main text and appendices A–O). Each paper acknowledges the other group's work as simultaneous, and we cite them together without a priority claim. All three are perturbative statements about formal power series. Benfatto, Pannell and Stergiou derive a Ward identity on $S^d$ that, under regularity and locality assumptions, gives a gradient formula with a manifestly symmetric metric that is positive definite at weak coupling, and makes the Jack–Osborn one-form removable in $d=2$ and $d=4$. Their construction leaves $\phi^3$ theory near six dimensions open and assumes, without proof, the locality of the anomaly for gauge couplings. Reehorst proves the gradient property for multiscalar $\phi^4$ theory in $d=4$ under two hypotheses (finiteness with local couplings and a remainder bound for large-momentum expansions). In his companion paper he derives regular gradient formulas near $d_c$ for four theories from one finiteness hypothesis each, together with a scheme with vanishing Jack–Osborn one-form for four-dimensional $\phi^4$ theory, while interacting gauge theories remain assumptions. R3 asks for a non-perturbative statement in $d=2$ or $d=4$, so its status stays "improved". We added three progress evidence items, a progress bullet and three references, recorded the authors' AI-use statements in a new provenance field, and rewrote the residual: it now says that the antisymmetric obstruction is removed perturbatively under these hypotheses, and that the non-perturbative question, including the Hartman–Mathys–Stergiou two-form, remains open. Checks: arXiv abstract pages show one version each, with no withdrawal and no journal reference. Searches of the-omega-institute/trureturing issues and pull requests for the three identifiers returned nothing, and keyword searches turned up only one unrelated issue (#6298). Not checked: the six-loop computations, the computer-algebra claims, and the long technical appendices of arXiv:2611.31050 beyond reading them (meromorphy, exclusion of lines of poles, finiteness on the sphere). The papers' comparisons with Poole–Thomsen and Pannell–Stergiou were not checked against those sources, and the catalogue text does not cite them.

**C13 (Open -> Improved).** Pirandola's arXiv:2611.29650v1 (22 November 2026, 21 pages, read in full including Appendices A–H) proves $Q_2\le K_2\le\log_2[1+(1-p)p^{p/(1-p)}]$ for the amplitude-damping channel, the Shannon capacity of the Z-channel obtained by preparing and measuring in the energy basis. The proof simulates fixed-excitation sectors exactly by teleportation and evaluates the relative entropy of entanglement of the sector resources exactly. We checked by hand the sector covariance, the conditional resource decomposition, the comparison state and its optimality argument, the identity $F_s(m)=I(S{:}T)\le s f_p(m/s)$, the block-gain inequality (D2), including the pinching cost $H(K)$, and the analytic comparisons of Appendix F. The replica and privacy-test steps are imported from Pirandola's earlier preprints arXiv:2610.06832v2 and 2610.08786v1, which were not read; they follow the standard PLOB framework. Numerically, the closed form agrees with $\max_x f_p(x)$. The bound lies strictly below the PLOB, max-relative-entropy and optimized balanced-squashing bounds on a 159-point grid and below the Fawzi–Shayeghi–Ta bound plotted in arXiv:2203.02127 (Fig. 2). At $p=1/2$ it gives $\log_2(5/4)$ against $\log_2(4/3)$, a 22.43% reduction. The high-damping ratio $e\ln3/3\approx0.99545$ is reproduced. For $s\le5$, the conditional resources have relative entropy $\log_2[\binom sk/\binom mk]$ with respect to the stated comparison state, and product vectors never exceed the claimed maximal overlap $\binom mk/\binom sk$. No known protocol exceeds the bound: we checked the reverse coherent information, the coherent information, triple rail, and the Hamming-weight-2 yields of arXiv:2410.22295, of which only Section 2 was read and which is not cited. The capacity remains undetermined at every $0<p<1$. The paper is v1, not peer reviewed, and its AI-use statement is recorded in provenance. **QF5 and QF11 (quantum field theory).** Two candidates were read from their arXiv sources (v1 of each, the only version; neither withdrawn). Fewster and Klein, *For whom the Bell tolls: joint measurements and state updates in quantum field theory*, [arXiv:2611.30468v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30468v1) (23 November 2026), was read in full, appendices included. **New entry F19 (foundations): sharp refinement of the Lieb–Solovej bound by Wigner negativity at Rényi order 1/2.**

- **Source and conjecture.** Gagatsos (arXiv:2611.30969v1, 23 November 2026; single version, not withdrawn) conjectures that every single-mode state satisfies ln‖W‖₁ ≤ h_{1/2}(Q) − ln(8π), with equality only for coherent states. Here ‖W‖₁ is the L¹ norm of the Wigner function and h_{1/2}(Q) = 2 ln ∫√Q is the Rényi-½ entropy of the Husimi function.
- **What the paper proves.**
  - It reduces the conjecture to pure states.
  - It proves the inequality with ln(4π), that is, up to an additive ln 2, for all states.
  - It shows that this constant is the best possible for any argument that treats the Wigner function as a generic two-mode wavefunction.
  - It exhibits a superposition |0⟩ + 0.2|3√2⟩ for which the analogous inequality fails at Rényi orders 1 (Wehrl), 2 and ∞.
- **Status chosen.** The entry is "Improved", with that partial result as progress. Horizon: sharp.
- **Reading.**
  - The paper was read in full from its LaTeX source, including Appendices A–D. Propositions 1–2, Lemmas 1–4 and the corollaries were checked line by line, and no error was found.
  - Also read in full, and cited in the entry:
    - Lieb–Solovej, Acta Math. 212 (2014), arXiv:1208.3632v2. Its Theorem 2.2 gives h_{1/2}(Q) ≥ ln 8π; the paper does not prove uniqueness of the minimizers.
    - Frank–Nicola–Tilli, arXiv:2307.14089v3. Their Theorem 3 with Φ(u) = −√u gives uniqueness and quadratic stability at order ½.
    - Albarelli–Genoni–Paris–Ferraro, PRA 98, 052350 (2018), arXiv:1804.05763v2: the Wigner logarithmic negativity as a monotone.
    - Kenfack–Życzkowski, J. Opt. B 6, 396 (2004), arXiv:quant-ph/0406015v1: the negative volume.
    - Cordero–Nicola, IMRN 2018, arXiv:1605.00481v3. In time–frequency language the conjecture asks for the sharp constant in the L¹ bound of the Wigner distribution by the squared Feichtinger-algebra norm. Cordero–Nicola cover the qualitative modulation-space bound with unspecified constants only.
- **Independent numerics.**
  - Method: Wigner functions by zero-padded fast Fourier transform, Husimi functions in exact Fock or coherent-overlap form. For Fock states, an exact one-dimensional Laguerre reduction against the closed form exp Δ_{1/2} = 2ⁿΓ(n/2+1)²/n!.
  - The counterexample is reproduced: ‖W‖₁ = 1.2268, excesses 0.3037, 0.1612, 0.0768 and 0.0392 at orders ½, 1, 2 and ∞ (paper: 1.227, 0.304, 0.161, 0.077, 0.039).
  - The Fock values ‖W_n‖₁ − 1 agree with Kenfack–Życzkowski's exact values to 10⁻⁵.
  - No violation of the conjecture was found for:
    - Fock states with n ≤ 300 (largest gap −0.097 at n = 1), and selected n up to 10⁴ (gap → about −0.37);
    - cat and three-component states;
    - squeezed Fock and squeezed cat states;
    - 300 Haar-random states per dimension 2–8 and 300 superpositions with the vacuum per dimension.
  - Nelder–Mead maximization in span{|0⟩,…,|3⟩}: 11 of 12 runs converged to coherent states, and one stopped at a local maximum with gap −0.027; no run found a positive gap.
  - An observation made here: the phase-invariant case is equivalent to the one-variable Laguerre inequality ½∫e^{-u/2}|L_n(u)|du ≤ 2ⁿΓ(n/2+1)²/n! for all n. Like the numerics above, it is not recorded in the catalogue, which keeps only the published results.
