# Selection of proposed problems · 19 November 2026

Of the 55 priority questions and 24 secondary leads previously listed, **49 priority questions and 21 secondary leads are retained**. Six priority questions and three secondary leads were withdrawn. The 218 active catalogue entries have a separate review process.

PM5 and PB2 have public proof claims matching the model and normalization. PH3 has explicit graph certificates, which we checked against the original target vectors. PM7 admits a counterexample. For PA9 the first two commonly used product formulas already give decisive worst-case benchmarks. PG2, SQF1, SO1 and SF2 need a distinct testable question before they warrant an independent listing. Their withdrawal is an editorial decision about scope.

Each row records why the question earns its place or why it was withdrawn. We read the published formulation and its earlier assessment, searched for work on each topic and compared the relevant models and quantifiers. Each row links to a source useful for that decision. The full bibliographies remain with the proposals. This AI-assisted selection does not certify the cited proofs or establish bibliographic priority. The specific calculations reproduced during the review follow the decisions.

### PM7 · an exact counterexample

Take the symbol $f(z_1,z_2)=z_1$. The image of the periodic torus is the unit circle, which has zero planar area, and its spectral measure is uniform on that circle. On a finite polygon each hop increases the first coordinate by one, up to the sign convention for the shift. After more hops than the horizontal span the matrix power vanishes. Its entire empirical eigenvalue measure is therefore a point mass at zero at every size. This answers the proposed measure equivalence negatively. Eigenvector localization in the source paper is a distinct criterion. [Zhang, Yang and Fang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05059).

### PA8 · why equality at the ODE threshold is a separate question

Use Euclidean vector norms and the induced matrix norm on the full tensor square in Liu et al.'s preparation-oracle model. Set $F_0=0$, $F_1=\operatorname{diag}(-1,-2)$ and $F_2=(1,b)^{\mathsf T}(1,0,0,0)$, with any fixed $b>0$. The linear part is normal, the spectral abscissa is $-1$, and for unit inputs $R=\sqrt{1+b^2}$. These matrices are identical in the two instances. The comparison below uses exact input norms of one. [Liu et al., Problem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.03185v4). [Lewis et al., non-normal comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09593).

Compare inputs $(1,0)$ and $(a,\sqrt{1-a^2})$ with $a=(1+e^{-T})^{-1}$. Both have bounded solutions throughout the interval. In the first case $x(T)=1$ and $y(T)=b(1-e^{-2T})/2$. In the second case $x(t)=(1+e^{t-T})^{-1}$, so $x(T)=1/2$. Variation of constants gives
$$
y(T)= e^{-2T}\sqrt{1-a^2}
     + b\int_0^T \frac{e^{-2s}}{(1+e^{-s})^2}\,ds
     \longrightarrow b(\log 2-1/2).
$$
Thus the limiting slopes of the two output vectors are $b/2$ and $b(2\log 2-1)$. Their normalized pure states have strictly positive trace distance for each fixed $b>0$, and both norm ratios are at most two.

Use a real two-dimensional rotation as the preparation oracle. The two rotations, and their inverses, differ in operator norm by $O(e^{-T/2})$. The hybrid argument replaces the queries one at a time, so constant distinguishing advantage requires $\Omega(e^{T/2})$ queries. Choosing the output tolerance to be a sufficiently small fixed fraction of the separation reduces this distinction to normalized-state preparation with bounded error (using a constant number of repetitions for a heralded solver). This rules out a uniform efficient region above one at fixed excess, even with two coordinates. At $b=0$ the limiting output directions coincide. The retained endpoint explicitly fixes $R=1$.

### PA9 · sharp benchmarks at low energy

Write $H=A+B$ and $C=[A,B]=[A,H]$. For the first-order product formula take $t\to0$ at fixed finite volume. Taylor expansion gives $\|CP_\Delta\|/2$ as the coefficient of $t^2$. Assume bounded site dimension, fixed support size and finite range, with per-site strength bounded by $g$ for the terms in the two layers. [Mizuta–Kuwahara, shifted low-energy estimate](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20746v1).

For any unit ground vector $\psi$, set $K=H-E_0$ and $m_j=\langle A\psi,K^jA\psi\rangle$. Then $\|C\psi\|^2=m_2\le\sqrt{m_1m_3}$ by the spectral theorem. Write $\operatorname{ad}_H X=[H,X]$. For odd $j$ one has $2m_j=\langle\psi,[A,\operatorname{ad}_H^j A]\psi\rangle$ by expanding the commutators on a ground vector. Only connected sequences of overlapping local terms contribute. Summing the norms, starting at any of the $N$ sites and using the per-site bound at each of the fixed number of steps, gives $m_1=O(Ng^3)$ and $m_3=O(Ng^5)$. This proves $\|CP_0\|=O(\sqrt N\,g^2)$, including degenerate ground spaces.

For the lower bound let $H$ be a sum of $m$ independent dimers, each with terms $X\otimes X$ and $Z\otimes I$ in opposite layers. Their commutator has norm two on every unit vector and anticommutes with the single-dimer Hamiltonian. It carries a dimer ground state into its excited space. Applying $C$ to a product of dimer ground states therefore gives $m$ orthogonal contributions of norm two. This realizes $\|CP_0\|/2\ge\sqrt m$ on $N=2m$ sites at fixed strength. The same lower bound applies to every spectral window that contains the ground space.

**A positive-width spectral window.** The upper bound also extends to all superpositions in the window, including gapless systems. Write $D=i[A,H]$, $K=H-E_0\ge0$, and $P_L=\mathbf1_{[0,L]}(K)$. Locality gives $\|[A,[A,H]]\|\le c_1Ng^3$ and $\|[D,[H,D]]\|\le c_2Ng^5$ with constants depending only on the locality bounds. Indeed, $D$ is a sum of overlapping pairs of terms with bounded range and per-site strength $O(g^2)$. For a unit $\phi$ in $P_L$, the expectation of $e^{isA}Ke^{-isA}$ is nonnegative for either sign of $s$, its initial value is at most $L$, and its second derivative is bounded by $c_1Ng^3$. Taylor's inequality optimized over $s$ gives $\|P_L D P_L\|^2\le 2c_1Ng^3L$.

Choose a unit eigenvector $\psi$ for the largest eigenvalue $M^2$ of $P_\Delta D^2P_\Delta$ on its range. Thus $M=\|DP_\Delta\|$. Since $K\psi$ is in the same range,
$$
\langle D\psi,KD\psi\rangle
= M^2\langle\psi,K\psi\rangle+\tfrac12\langle\psi,[D,[H,D]]\psi\rangle
\le \Delta M^2+c_2Ng^5/2.
$$
Take $L=2(\Delta+g)$ with $g>0$. The low-energy part of $D\psi$ has squared norm at most $2c_1Ng^3L$ by the compression estimate. The squared norm above $L$ is at most $(\Delta M^2+c_2Ng^5/2)/L$ by positivity of $K$. Adding these inequalities and absorbing $\Delta M^2/L\le M^2/2$ gives $M^2=O(Ng^3(\Delta+g))$. At fixed $\Delta/g$ the square-root size dependence is sharp, by the dimer example.

**Symmetric second-order splitting.** On a single dimer denote its two terms by $a,b$, so $a^2=b^2=I$ and $ab=-ba$. The symmetric product equals $\cos(t)e^{-ita}-i\sin(t)b$, while the exact propagator equals $\cos(\sqrt2t)I-i\sin(\sqrt2t)(a+b)/\sqrt2$. Their difference has cubic term $-it^3(-a/3+b/6)$. In a ground state of $a+b$, both term expectations are $-1/\sqrt2$. The absolute value of the cubic coefficient of the diagonal error is therefore $1/(6\sqrt2)$. Tensoring $m$ dimers adds the leading coefficients, producing a lower bound $m/(6\sqrt2)$ in the stated vector norm. The corresponding upper bound is linear in site count by the local nested-commutator estimate. Here the overall phase contributes to the norm. Observable error, error up to phase, accumulated error over many steps and the scaling on a fixed interacting chain are separate targets.

These are elementary short-time comparisons for the proposal's stated norm. They do not classify all product formulas or prove optimal finite-time simulation costs.
