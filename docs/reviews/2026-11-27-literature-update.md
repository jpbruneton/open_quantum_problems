## F18 · issue #28 (Ximing Wang, `vexpaer`): Lean verified

The issue follows up on #23, where the “Lean verified” badge was withheld because the Stratonovich–Weyl kernels were constructed in Lean only for $j=\tfrac12$ and the build had not been reproduced. The repository [`vexpaer/su2-wigner-negativity`](https://github.com/vexpaer/su2-wigner-negativity/tree/ac94e7985878e2c5e51cca02bc1c9120d0c23161) was checked at commit `ac94e79`.

**Build.** `lake exe cache get`, `lake build` (6126 jobs) and `lake env lean Audit.lean` were run locally with Lean 4.36.0 and succeed. The audit, which calls `collectAxioms` and fails on any axiom outside `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, passes for all sixteen listed declarations, including `pi_ortho_all`, `universal_wigner_negativity`, `canonicalStratonovichWeyl` and `canonical_universal_wigner_negativity`. The author's GitHub Actions workflow passes on the same commit with identical audit output. A text search of the Lean sources finds no `sorry`, `admit`, `axiom`, `native_decide`, `implemented_by` or `opaque`.

**Statements.** The structure `StratonovichWeylSystem N` (`WignerNegativity.lean`, line 372) has four fields. `poly` requires each $W_\psi$ to be a real polynomial on $S^2$. `norm_eq` requires $\frac{N+1}{4\pi}\int W_\psi=\langle\psi|\psi\rangle$. `covariant` requires $W_{D(g)\psi}(R_g u)=W_\psi(u)$ for the explicit spin-$N/2$ representation `spinRep`. `tracial` requires $\frac{N+1}{4\pi}\int W_\varphi W_\psi=|\langle\varphi|\psi\rangle|^2$ for all $\varphi,\psi$. These are the defining properties of a self-dual SU(2)-covariant Wigner function. The kernel in the statement of F18 satisfies them, as does every sign choice of the self-dual kernel. `universal_wigner_negativity` proves that every unit pure state has a negative value for every such system, for every $N\ge1$, so its statement is stronger than F18's. The new file `CoherentGram.lean` constructs an instance for every $N$: the Gram matrix $G$ of the coherent-state symbols on Hermitian matrices is shown to be positive definite and to commute with the adjoint action, and $G^{-1/2}$ applied to the symbols is shown to satisfy the four fields. The axioms are therefore not vacuous, which was the objection in #23. The structure does not require $W_\psi$ to be quadratic in $\psi$; this weakens the hypotheses only.

**What is not formalized.** For $j\ge\tfrac32$, Lean does not prove that the Clebsch–Gordan kernel $\Delta_{j,m}=\sum_l\frac{2l+1}{2j+1}\langle j,m;l,0|j,m\rangle$ of the statement is an instance. This identification is formalized for $j=\tfrac12$ and $j=1$ (`spinOne_eq_diagonal`). The author states this limitation in the issue. Since the formal theorem quantifies over every kernel with the Stratonovich–Weyl properties, the remaining step is that the Clebsch–Gordan kernel has those properties, which is its defining feature.

**Decision.** With the maintainer's agreement, F18 receives the “Lean verified” badge. Its note records the scope: every spin, every kernel satisfying the four axioms, and the Clebsch–Gordan identification only for $j=\tfrac12$ and $j=1$. The claim text and the evidence summary of Wang's proof are updated, and the AI-use statement of the issue (Antigravity / Gemini and Codex) is added to provenance. F18 stays “Claimed solved” and stays closed to further proofs; this is a formalization of a proof already listed, not a new proof. Wang is listed in the new Contributors section of `README.md`.

## M18 · issue #27 (Yifan Zhang, `nylxiperia`)

**Open → Improved.** The issue and its two follow-up comments report three Zenodo versions of “Partial Spectral Bounds for the Quantum Rabi Model” (v1.2.0, doi:10.5281/zenodo.24564980; v1.3.0, doi:10.5281/zenodo.24575757; v1.4.0, doi:10.5281/zenodo.24582915, all three by Yifan Zhang). The PDF and the proof-artifact archive of each version were downloaded from the Zenodo API; their checksums match the records. The manuscript source, in Markdown (`manuscript/technical-note.md` of the v1.4.0 archive, Git commit `17abf038`, no public repository), was read in full. Inequalities are numbered as in the entry, in units $\omega=1$, with each parity block written as $H_t=N+g(a+a^\dagger)-t(-1)^N$, $t=\pm\Delta$; this reduction was checked against the full Hamiltonian split by parity (agreement to $4\times10^{-14}$).

*Gap inequality (2).* The proof (section “The same-parity gap bound”) is a commutator sum rule with trial vectors $(a^\dagger)^2\psi_k$ and $a^2\psi_k$ projected off the first $n+1$ eigenvectors; strictness follows from a positive term $S_n=-\tfrac g2\sum_k\langle\psi_k,X\psi_k\rangle$, which cannot vanish because the eigenvector span would then be invariant under both $N$ and $X$. Domains, both commutator identities, the cancellation of finite blocks, the norm identity from $[a^2,a^{\dagger2}]=4N+2$ and the final bound $E_{n+1}-E_n\le2-4S_n/C_n<2$ were rederived. No gap was found; the argument holds for either sign of $g$, every $\Delta$ and every $n$. It is identical in v1.3 and v1.4.

*Parity-splitting inequality (1).* New in v1.4. The reduction, the case $|t|\le\tfrac12$ (Hellmann–Feynman), the positive elimination region, the head/tail Schur-complement framework, the three closing tests, the two-coordinate head-extension recurrence and its termination, the Gram and endpoint formulas, and the separate $n=0$ proof were followed. Three compressed lemmas were not rederived symbol by symbol: the weighted row-dominance proofs of the two Gram-difference positivity claims (manuscript lines 89–102 and 120–124), the finite-interval recurrence (138–172) and the odd last interval (174–202). Their intermediate claims were tested on about 400,000 random head pairs, and the full proof procedure was run on 84,136 Rabi cases; there was no failure.

*Third inequality (3).* $n=1$ is analytic. For $n=2$ and $n=3$ the proof is computer-assisted: large detuning uses even or odd Fock states, strong coupling the first $n+1$ displaced states (exact margins 0.079 and 0.139 against the 0.5 required), and the intermediate range for $n=3$ a polynomial matrix pencil certified with Arb for all real $t$ at once; the $n=2$ boxes and $n=3$ intervals cover the parameter domain, and the seams overlap. The weak-coupling threshold for every $n$ is explicit (about $10^{-63}$ for $n=2$, $10^{-126}$ for $n=4$). The passage from finite certificates to the operator, by min–max over finite-support or displaced trial vectors, is written, not formalized, and is correct. The three certificate scripts pass (python-flint 0.9.0; 12.8 s, 4.6 s and 154 s). The five Lean files compile with Lean 4.36.0 and the pinned Mathlib in about 5.5 minutes, contain no `sorry` or `native_decide`, and their main theorems depend only on the standard axioms. For (3), v1.3 and v1.4 are byte-identical, and v1.2 differs only by one coverage paragraph.

*Independent numerics.* Tridiagonal diagonalization (truncation stable to $6\times10^{-13}$) on 10,734 points with $g\le15$ and $|\Delta|\le200$, 40 levels each, and on Lanuza's three Braak counterexample points, found no violation of (1) or (2); the smallest margins, about $2\times10^{-6}$, occur at $g=0.02$, $\Delta=200$, as expected near $g=0$. For (3) with $n\le6$, and at every resonance dip for $n\le8$ over 206 values of $g$ (rechecked with 60-digit arithmetic), no violation was found; for $n=2$ the margin at small-coupling resonances is $0.1718\,g^5$.

*Priority.* No antecedent in the-omega-institute/trureturing (2503.15572, Braak, Lanuza, Rabi, Juddian, M18; issues and pull requests, open and closed, and code search), in nine GitHub repository searches, or on arXiv. Lanuza's paper has versions v1–v3 and is not withdrawn. The working paper's bibliography cites only Lanuza, this catalogue and the author's own records.

*Decision.* With the maintainer's agreement, the working paper is listed as progress and M18 moves from Open to Improved. The third inequality remains open for $n\ge4$ outside the weak-coupling and large-detuning regimes, and the parity-splitting lemmas still need an independent check. There is no Lean badge, since the contribution is partial progress; the evidence item records the Lean formalization of the finite statements. The AI-use statement of the issue is recorded in provenance, and Zhang is listed in the Contributors section of `README.md`.

## B8 · Yang, nontrivial three-dimensional quantum cellular automata

Yang, “Three-dimensional quantum cellular automata that are not stably circuits”, [arXiv:2610.40235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40235v2) (v1 29 October 2026, v2 3 November 2026, “reference added”; not withdrawn), was read in full from the v2 TeX source, proofs included, and compared with v1: Sections 2–6 are identical, and v2 adds references and a remark and drops one introductory sentence. The general theory (well-definedness by cylinder gluing, vanishing on trivial subalgebras, invariance, additivity) was rederived by hand, and the decisive determinant of Section 6 was recomputed independently with sympy: it equals $-1/x_1$, so the half-plane index is $p^{-1}$ and the invariant is the nonzero class of $p$ in $\mathbb Q_{>0}/\mathbb Q_{>0}^2$ for every odd prime $p$. The subalgebra used coincides with that of Sun, Yang, Wang, Tantivasadakarn and Chen (arXiv:2509.07099v3, the relevant section read). The cited Brauer-group definitions and boundary-algebra lemmas of Haah (arXiv:2211.02086v4) were read in part. No gap was found. The result is unconditional, unlike the earlier three-fermion argument, which assumed that no two-dimensional commuting-projector three-fermion model exists. It covers bounded-spread automata on $\mathbb Z^3$ with finite-dimensional sites, not $p=2$, the three-fermion automaton or quasi-local automata, and it concerns automorphisms, not gapped Hamiltonian phases. It is therefore recorded as progress on B8, whose status stays Improved. Its Conjecture 7.4 bears on B9 only conditionally; B9 is unchanged. Kwan, Long and Haah (arXiv:2609.09317v1, introduction read) announce a separate nontriviality proof for the three-fermion automaton, which is not on arXiv as of 27 November 2026 and is not recorded. No antecedent was found in the-omega-institute/trureturing or on arXiv. The AI-use statement at the end of Section 1 is recorded in provenance.

## New entry M21 · Berry–Tabor conjecture for flat tori

The Berry–Tabor conjecture was mentioned only inside M9. The new entry asks whether the triple correlation of the Laplace spectrum of almost every flat two-torus is Poissonian, a question stated as open by Aistleitner, Blomer and Radziwiłł (arXiv:1809.07881v1, §1.2: “nothing is known for the case of correlations of higher order, that is, for $n>2$”), by Blomer and Li (arXiv:2211.02469v2, §1) and by Kim, Marklof and Welsh (arXiv:2601.03209v2, §1.1). The spacing law was not chosen as the target because Aistleitner–Blomer–Radziwiłł and Blomer–Li describe it as far out of reach; it is kept in the residual. Read in full and cited: Kim–Marklof–Welsh (v2), Aistleitner–Blomer–Radziwiłł (v1, JEMS 26 (2024)), Blomer–Bourgain–Radziwiłł–Rudnick (arXiv:1604.02413v3, Ann. Sci. ÉNS 50 (2017)), Eskin–Margulis–Mozes (Ann. of Math. 161 (2005), free PDF from the journal), Lutsko (arXiv:2609.03112v1) and Blomer–Li (v2, IMRN 2023). Versions and withdrawal status were checked, and journal details were checked on Crossref. Berry–Tabor (1977), Sarnak (1997) and VanderKam (1999, 2000) could not be read and are listed in `manual_todo.md`, not cited. No overlap with other entries, and no antecedent in trureturing. Lutsko's AI-use declaration and the Blomer–Li v2 comment on AI-found inaccuracies are recorded in provenance. M9 now links to M21.
