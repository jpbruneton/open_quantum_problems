## Scope and method

**arXiv.** Queried the arXiv export API on 26 October 2026 for submissions dated 14–23 October in quant-ph, math-ph, cond-mat.str-el, hep-th and cs.CC: 628, 288, 194, 369 and 81 records respectively, 1,370 unique records after merging cross-lists. Manuscripts already cited in the catalogue were checked for new versions: 107 cited arXiv identifiers from August–October were queried and four had new versions after 14 October (see below).

**OpenAI Math Release.** Reviewed [`openai/math`](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/7ef5c0cc5ca4b0e072b7a050fca09ab24bc6fa3a) at commit `7ef5c0c` (26 October 2026), the first change since the `1581d4c` commit reviewed on 17 October. ## OpenAI Math Release: N2 verification package

The 17 October review recorded that the $M(6)=3$ claim rests on a computer-assisted exclusion under stated binary64 conditions and that the pipeline was not public. The 26 October commit adds, under the manuscript directory, `verification/saved-run/VERIFY.md`, `compiled-build/source/` (seven C++ files), `execution/data/` (binary records), `execution/commands.json`, `execution/results/` and `execution/check_records.py`. The README states that the records "match the October 4 run" and that `VERIFY.md` supersedes the package instructions on pages 49–54 of the manuscript.

What the package establishes, in OpenAI's own words: the checker is "a numerical consistency check" of the saved records (complete parsing, shard and batch counts, ordered failure subsequences, final modulo-four coverage), not an authentication of the archive; the build script compiles the programs but does not reproduce the full computation; and the correctness of the C++ implementation and of compiler and hardware behaviour "are separate questions". The binary64 conditions (round-to-nearest, correctly rounded square root, `FLT_EVAL_METHOD == 0`, no fast-math or contraction) are stated as requirements on any fresh run.

Implementation: a `numerical` evidence item pinned to commit `7ef5c0c`, a provenance item for the Lattice24 audit, a context paragraph and two references. The manuscript evidence remains pinned to `1581d4c`. Status unchanged.

## Screened but not incorporated

- Becker–Yang, [Lenses make many points: Pure point spectrum disproves Simon's $L^2$ conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31637v1) (22 October), constructs, "with LLM-assistance" as the abstract states, lens-type potentials with pure point spectrum on $(0,\infty)$, refuting Problem 8 of Simon's 2000 list. - Chiba–Hamazaki (Hilbert-subspace cloning), Matsui (invariant-subspace hierarchy) and Ccuiro–Fazio–Silva (dissipative scars) construct new nonergodic mechanisms; B7 asks for stability theorems under perturbations, which they do not supply.
